<h3 style="color:var(--accent,#c9a94e)">Quick Answer</h3>
    <p><strong>Keep your golf cart running with four habits: charge the battery after every use, check tire pressure monthly (18–22 psi), wash off salt and sand weekly, and store it dry with a charged battery</strong>. Electric carts need minimal maintenance — no oil changes, no gas — so these simple steps cover most of what a cart owner needs to do. If you're visiting Destin, skip the maintenance entirely: every cart we rent is brand new and serviced before delivery.</p>

    <h3>Battery Care: The #1 Maintenance Task</h3>
    <p>Your battery is the heart of an electric cart. Charge after every use — even short trips — and avoid letting it sit below 50% charge for long periods. Plug in overnight with the charger that came with your cart, and never leave it plugged in for weeks at a time without checking it.</p>

    <h3>Tire Pressure and Tread</h3>
    <p>Most golf cart tires run <strong>18–22 psi</strong> — check the sidewall for the exact recommendation. Under-inflated tires drain battery faster and wear unevenly. Check monthly, and look for cracks or bald spots, especially on carts that sit in the Florida sun.</p>

    <h3>Cleaning: Salt, Sand, and Sun</h3>
    <p>Coastal air is tough on carts. Rinse off salt and sand after beach trips, wipe down seats and dashboards, and use a UV protectant on vinyl seats. Keep the battery compartment dry and free of debris.</p>

    <h3>Seasonal Storage</h3>
    <p>If you store a cart for the season: clean it, inflate tires to the top of the recommended range, store in a dry covered space, and keep the battery at 50–100% charge with a maintenance charger. In spring, check tire pressure, brakes, and battery water levels before your first ride.</p>
