    <h3>Florida E-Bike Laws: The 30-Second Version</h3>
    <p>Florida law (Statute 316.20655) recognizes three e-bike classes. Class 1 is pedal-assist only, Class 2 has a throttle, and Class 3 is pedal-assist up to 28 mph. For you as a renter, the practical rules are simple:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>No license, registration, or insurance</strong> is required — e-bikes are treated like bicycles, not mopeds.</li>
      <li><strong>Riders under 16 must wear a helmet.</strong> Riders 16 and up do not have to, but we still hand you one.</li>
      <li><strong>You must be 16 or older to ride a Class 3 e-bike.</strong> Most rental fleets stick to Class 1 and Class 2 bikes, which is exactly why — simpler rules, friendlier for families.</li>
      <li><strong>Ride where bicycles ride:</strong> roads, bike lanes, shoulders, and multi-use paths. E-bikes are allowed anywhere regular bicycles are allowed under Florida law.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Local rule worth knowing: <strong>motorized vehicles — including e-bikes — are not allowed on the sandy beach</strong> in Destin and the surrounding beaches. That is a beach, not a bike path. Push the bike across the sand to your spot or lock it at the beach access. And on multi-use paths like the Timpoochee Trail, keep speeds civil — those paths carry walkers, strollers, and runners, and nothing ruins a vacation like a heel-strike collision.</p>

    <h3>Where to Ride: The Best E-Bike Routes Near Destin</h3>
    <p>This is the part people underestimate. An e-bike is not just transport — it unlocks routes that would be miserable on a regular bike in August heat.</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>The Timpoochee Trail (30A).</strong> Roughly 19 miles of paved, mostly car-free path running from Dune Allen Beach to Inlet Beach through a dozen coastal communities — Seaside, WaterColor, Rosemary Beach, Alys Beach. This is the crown jewel ride of Walton County, and the motor means you can do a big chunk of it even with zero cycling fitness. Stop for lunch in Seaside, ride on.</li>
      <li><strong>Henderson Beach State Park, Destin.</strong> A mile-long paved road plus easy access from the multi-use paths along US 98. Bicyclists pay a small entry fee (a few dollars per person — cars pay $6 per vehicle). Shaded campsites, real dunes, and the quietest stretch of shoreline in Destin.</li>
      <li><strong>Destin Harbor Boardwalk.</strong> Easy, flat, and full of reasons to stop: boat tours, seafood, ice cream, people watching. Ride over in the morning before the crowds, or at sunset when the harbor turns gold.</li>
      <li><strong>Okaloosa Island.</strong> The multi-use path along Santa Rosa Boulevard connects beach accesses, the fishing pier, and The Boardwalk on the Island. Wide, flat, and far less traffic-crazy than US 98 in Destin proper.</li>
      <li><strong>Sunset cruise on your own power.</strong> Our favorite cheap date: grab the bikes an hour before sunset, ride west along the beach road, and watch the sky do its thing from any public beach access. See our date-ideas roundup for more.</li>
    </ul>
